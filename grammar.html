Section 8 — Grammar Guide

5 practical English grammar rules used in professional software environments. Click any card for detailed examples.

Grammar 1
Present Simple
Used for habitual actions, general truths, and recurring processes in tech workflows.
Subject + base verb [+ s/es]
  • She reviews pull requests every morning.
  • The API returns a JSON response.
  • We deploy twice per sprint.
Pitfall: Add -s/-es for he/she/it: She works, He codes.

Grammar 2
Present Perfect
Connects past actions to the present moment — essential for technical status updates and handoffs.
Subject + have/has + past participle
  • We have deployed the new feature.
  • The client has approved the mockups.
  • I have fixed the critical bug.
Pitfall: Don't use with specific past time: ❌ I have finished at 3 PM.

Grammar 3
Present Perfect Continuous
Highlights the duration and continuity of an action — ideal for describing ongoing development or investigations.
Subject + have/has + been + V-ing
  • We have been migrating the database for 2h.
  • She has been debugging the auth module.
  • The team has been testing for a week.
Key Use: Emphasizes process duration, not just result.

Grammar 4
Future Simple — Will
Used for promises, commitments, predictions, and spontaneous decisions. Essential in client-facing communication.
Subject + will + base verb
  • I will notify the team right away.
  • The system will auto-scale at peak load.
  • We will deliver the MVP by Friday.
Pitfall: ❌ I will to go → ✅ I will go (no "to" after will).

Grammar 5
Modal Verbs for Politeness
Modals like could, would, may, shall signal formality level. Choosing the right modal is essential in investor and client interactions.
Modal (could/would/may/shall) + base verb
  • Could you review this document?
  • May I assist you with that?
  • Would you mind waiting a moment?
  • Shall we proceed to the boardroom?
Formality Scale: can → could → would → may → shall (most formal)
